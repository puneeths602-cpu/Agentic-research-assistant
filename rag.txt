Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds an LLM answer in external documents. Documents are split into chunks, embedded as vectors and stored in a vector database such as Chroma or FAISS.
At query time the question is embedded, the nearest chunks are retrieved by cosine similarity and passed to the model as context, which reduces hallucination and allows citing sources.
Chunk size matters: chunks that are too large dilute relevance, and chunks that are too small lose context. Overlap between chunks helps preserve meaning at the boundaries.
RAG quality is measured with retrieval metrics such as recall at k and with generation metrics such as groundedness, which checks that claims are supported by retrieved context.
